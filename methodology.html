Methodology & Sources

LAWvalues 2.0 is a transparent, research-grounded beta instrument. Its eight categories are preserved. The scoring structure has been rebuilt so that each question measures one category rather than moving several scores at once.

Construct Map

Each axis represents a distinct jurisprudential disagreement. The dimensions are defined before the questions, and every question is assigned to exactly one dimension.

Scoring

Each axis has six questions: three keyed toward the first pole and three toward the second. Responses run from −3 to +3. “Not sure / skip” is missing rather than a midpoint answer.

axis score = 50 + 50 × Σ(item key × response) / (3 × answered items)

A score of 100 is the first pole, 0 is the second, and 50 is the midpoint. Balanced keying means that giving the same agreement response to every question produces 50 rather than an artificial direction.

Why the Scores Separate

One axis per question

A response about legal validity no longer changes remedies, precedent, institutional posture, and form by assumption. Cross-axis relationships must come from respondents, not from the scoring key.

Balanced pole wording

Half of each scale is stated from each pole. This reduces simple agreement bias while avoiding confusing double negatives.

Interleaved administration

The quiz randomizes six rounds containing one question from every axis. Respondents are not shown the measured category while answering.

Calibration before classification

Jurist, school, and champion labels remain disabled until pilot data show how the questions behave and where defensible profiles belong.

Validation Status

Established: balanced coverage, source provenance, one-axis loading, scoring symmetry, midpoint neutrality under indiscriminate agreement, and automated structural tests.

Not yet established: factor structure in actual respondents, internal reliability, test–retest stability, convergent and discriminant validity, subgroup measurement invariance, or calibrated jurist and movement locations.

The next stage is expert review and cognitive interviewing, followed by a heterogeneous pilot sample and item-level analysis. Classification should follow that evidence rather than being invented in advance.
