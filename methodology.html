Instrument 2.0

Methodology and sources

LAWvalues is a transparent, content-grounded beta instrument. It improves structural separation among the eight dimensions; empirical validation requires pilot data and is not claimed yet.

Construct map

The dimensions are defined before the items. Each question is assigned to one dimension and cannot directly move any other score.

Scoring

Balanced single-axis scoring

Each axis has six items. Three are keyed toward the first pole and three toward the second. Responses run from −3 to +3. “Not sure” is missing rather than zero.

axis score = 50 + 50 × Σ(item key × response) / (3 × answered items)

A score of 100 is the first pole, 0 is the second, and 50 is the midpoint. Because keying is balanced, giving the same agreement response to every item produces 50 rather than an artificial directional result.

Structural safeguards

No cross-loading by fiat

The prior instrument let one answer add points to many axes. Version 2 gives every item one axis and one key, so a source-of-law response cannot mechanically alter remedies or separation of powers.

Opposite-pole wording

Half of each scale is written from each pole. The items state rival propositions rather than merely inserting negations, reducing acquiescence without making the wording needlessly confusing.

Interleaved administration

Question order is randomized in six rounds, with one item from every axis per round. Respondents are not told which dimension a question measures while answering.

Validation status

What is established, and what remains

Established now: balanced coverage, source provenance, one-axis loading, scoring symmetry, midpoint neutrality under indiscriminate agreement, and automated audit tests.

Not established yet: factor structure in actual respondents, internal reliability, test–retest stability, convergent and discriminant validity, subgroup measurement invariance, or calibrated locations for jurists and movements.

Scale-development research treats item generation, pretesting, factor extraction, reliability, and validity as distinct stages. The next defensible step is expert review and cognitive interviewing, followed by a heterogeneous pilot sample and item-level analysis. Profile labels should follow, not precede, that work.
