# LAWvalues Matching Architecture

The eight public axes remain the instrument. Jurisprudential identities and thinker matches are an interpretive layer built on top of the 93 core responses plus, when useful, no more than seven adaptive tie-breakers.

## Sparse signatures, not eight-number caricatures

A thinker is not assigned a position on every axis. Each selectable thinker has:

- **modeled axes**: only the dimensions that materially characterize the thinker;
- **weights**: how diagnostic those modeled dimensions are;
- **signature traits**: question-level ideas that make the thinker distinctive;
- **required traits / gates**: evidence that must exist before a specialist can win;
- **anti-traits**: strong contrary evidence that should suppress the match;
- **tier**: main, specialist, catch-all, or lineage.

An absent axis is unknown or non-diagnostic, never an implicit 50.

Matching therefore uses the *shape* of a respondent's profile. Two people can sit on the same side of every axis but differ because one respondent's strongest feature is Textualism while another's is Positivism. A specialist can beat a broad profile when the respondent has the specialist's distinctive spike.

## Three substantive levels

### 1. Major jurisprudential families

A major family remains available even if most of its distinction is concentrated on one axis. Do not prune a real jurisprudential tradition merely because another thinker occupies similar eight-axis space.

Current protected families include:

- Legal Positivism
- Natural Law
- Dworkinian jurisprudence (with Interpretivism retained only as an internal parent where useful)
- Greenbergian Moral Impact Theory / one-system nonpositivism
- Legal Realism
- Pragmatism
- Textualism / Intentionalism / Purposivism
- Legal Process
- Originalism and its genuinely different subtypes
- Living Constitutionalism
- Common-Law Constitutionalism
- Common-Good Constitutionalism when directly diagnosed
- Thayerian, Waldronian, Popular-Constitutionalist, Departmentalist, Elyan, and Minimalist accounts of judicial review
- Formalist and Functionalist separation of powers
- Structured Proportionality

Natural Law is specifically protected from collapsing into generic Non-Positivism. Finnis is a main modern champion; Fuller and Radbruch require their narrower distinctive evidence. Dworkin likewise is not simply "the non-positivist."

### 2. Diagnosable specialists

A specialist deserves a selectable personal result when a narrow, judicially meaningful idea can distinguish that person from nearby broad results.

Examples:

- Lawrence Solum: public meaning / principle application / construction-zone nuance
- Jack Balkin: fixed framework plus legitimate later construction
- Frederick Schauer: unusually strong commitment to rules as constraints on decision-makers
- James Bradley Thayer: clear-error judicial deference
- Robert Alexy: champion-level fit for Proportionalism when non-positivist correctness claims accompany structured proportionality
- Owen Fiss: structural remedies and continuing supervision
- Roscoe Pound: sociological jurisprudence—law in action, competing social interests, and practical social operation
- Henry Hart & Albert Sacks: Legal Process—purpose plus institutional competence, settlement, and reasoned elaboration

A specialist never wins from coordinate proximity alone.

### 3. Lineage and further reading

Important figures can appear on result pages without being eligible to win.

Savigny and the Historical School are the model case. The tradition is major jurisprudential history, but LAWvalues' Historical/Living axis concerns fixation of constitutional meaning, not Savigny's organic development through custom and legal culture. Savigny is therefore further reading from compatible tradition/common-law results rather than a scored endpoint.

This tier also accommodates academics whose work is illuminating but too narrow, insufficiently diagnosable, or redundant with a stronger selectable personality.

## Catch-all without false theory

Sandra Day O'Connor is a deliberate exception to theory-first matching. A broadly moderate respondent who repeatedly prefers fact-sensitive balancing, narrow decisions, precedent, and case-by-case judgment may genuinely be better described by O'Connor's low-theory judicial style than by forcing an elaborate jurisprudential system onto them.

Philip Bobbitt covers a different apparent-middle case: principled methodological pluralism. The adaptive bank distinguishes "several modalities are legitimately available" from "I do not want a grand theory and would decide concretely."

## Adaptive diagnostics

The core always determines the eight displayed scores. Adaptive questions only preserve distinctions compressed by those eight aggregates.

The first follow-up is a soft Legal Realism probe because Realism is intentionally outside the normative axes. The second is a simple response check that never affects jurisprudential scores. Later questions are routed only when they can discriminate live candidates. Extremely fast completion, straight-line responding, excessive skipping, or a failed response check withholds the thinker/result classification rather than turning low-information clicking into a centrist or low-theory match. Examples include:

- Public Meaning vs Original Intent vs Original Methods vs Framework Originalism
- Inclusive vs Exclusive Positivism
- Natural Law vs Dworkinian Interpretivism vs Moral Impact Theory vs Fuller/Radbruch edge cases
- Thayerian deference vs Waldronian democratic legitimacy
- Popular Constitutionalism vs Departmentalism
- Intentionalism vs Purposivism
- Legal Process vs generic Purposivism/Pragmatism
- Pound-style sociological jurisprudence
- structured Proportionality
- Bobbitt-style pluralism vs O'Connor-style low theory

The router stops when no remaining diagnostic is materially useful or after seven adaptive questions.

## Public result grammar

After identity selection, the public result is assembled as **[noun-conditioned twist] [real jurisprudential identity] [broad residual phrase]**.

The three slots now do different jobs:

1. **Noun:** the respondent's nearest serious jurisprudential identity. This is the primary classification.
2. **Phrase:** the strongest broad characteristic not already communicated by the noun. Phrase eligibility is deliberately conservative: the public pool consists of the eight axis poles plus `objective_morality` and `anti_discretion`, the two narrower traits that the existing core/diagnostic bank can support with genuine breadth. A one-off answer cannot become a phrase.
3. **Modifier / twist:** a conditional refinement of the noun, not the third-highest global trait. It asks: *given that this person is a [noun], what is unusually informative about the particular kind of [noun] they are?* Twists may therefore use narrower subtype or adaptive-diagnostic evidence when that evidence genuinely distinguishes variants inside or around the selected noun.

The modifier layer is deliberately **between noun and champion** in granularity. It should capture a real sub-umbrella or meaningful twist shared by multiple plausible champions, not merely rename the runner-up noun and not reproduce an individual scholar's signature doctrine. If no measured subtype genuinely improves on the noun, the result uses an explicit archetypal/default modifier rather than inventing fake precision. Alexy-shaped proportionality and Bickel-shaped minimalism are model cases: **Proportionalist** and **Judicial Minimalist** stay the nouns; the finer distinction moves to modifier/champion level.

This distinction matters. A Solum-like respondent may be a **Public-Meaning Originalist** whose strongest broad residual characteristic is textual constraint, while the front modifier captures the more specific fixed-meaning / construction-zone package that makes that originalism Solum-like. The title can therefore say something like **Elbow-room Public-Meaning Originalist ...** rather than spending both public joke slots on the respondent's second- and third-largest axis bars.

Phrase selection still spends noun redundancy. Twist selection is noun-conditioned and also suppresses a twist when the trailing phrase already communicates the same concept. High-value cross-axis combinations may additionally produce an explanatory bridge paragraph. Closest-thinker matching remains a separate later step.

## False-match rule

Thinker matching is conservative. Numerical closeness is only the start.

A result must not hand someone a thinker when the respondent rejects the proposition that makes that thinker distinctive. Required traits and anti-traits operate as eligibility/falsification checks. Ambiguous top matches are pluralized; weak matches return no single thinker.

This is particularly important where the eight axes compress disputes that are large in substantive philosophy but outside LAWvalues' subject. The quiz does not add separate dimensions for capabilities, hierarchy/domination, distributive justice, equality theory, or political ideology merely to separate people who happen to share adjudicative methods.

## Calibration

The current locations and thresholds are theory-derived prototypes. Pilot responses should later calibrate:

- axis targets and weights;
- specialist gates;
- identity thresholds;
- diagnostic routing frequency;
- ambiguity margins;
- catch-all frequency.

Calibration may move numbers. It should not erase the structural rules above without an explicit redesign.
