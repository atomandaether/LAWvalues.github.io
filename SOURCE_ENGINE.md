# Source-of-law classification engine

This file records the locked algorithmic architecture for the Source / nature-of-law result zone. Public explanatory copy can change later without changing this taxonomy.

## Public nouns

The classifier may publicly return:

- **Positivist**
- **Natural Lawyer**
- **Interpretivist**
- **Fullerian**
- **Non-Positivist** only as the residual fallback when positivism is rejected but no narrower public Source noun is established.

The following remain internal/legacy compatibility records rather than public nouns:

- Inclusive Positivist
- Exclusive Positivist
- Dworkinian
- Moral Impact Theory
- Radbruchian / Extreme-Injustice theory

## Modifier layer

### Positivist

Substantive modifiers:

- **Inclusive Positivist** — a source-grounded legal system may incorporate genuine moral criteria into its rules of legal validity.
- **Exclusive Positivist** — legal validity must remain identifiable through social/source facts rather than moral merits themselves.
- **Normative Positivist** — source-based legality is defended as an institutional and democratic virtue that limits merits-based judicial lawmaking.

Normative Positivism is orthogonal to the Inclusive/Exclusive conceptual dispute. The public modifier engine shows the strongest supported substantive branch rather than forcing every Positivist into a complete partition.

### Non-Positivist fallback

Substantive descriptive modifiers:

- **Moral-Impact Non-Positivist** → Mark Greenberg.
- **Moral-Practice Non-Positivist** → Scott Hershovitz.
- **Extreme-Injustice Non-Positivist** → Gustav Radbruch / Philip Soper.

These modifiers deliberately describe the jurisprudential theory rather than repeating the champion's surname.

### Natural Lawyer / Interpretivist / Fullerian

No substantive modifier is currently locked. These nouns use the generic fit modifier unless later research reveals a genuine intermediate camp that changes the jurist type.

## Generic fit modifier

When no substantive modifier applies:

- **Unmistakably a [noun]** or **Unmistakably an [vowel-initial noun]** for an unusually clean, well-separated match.
- **[noun]** for the deliberately narrow middle band.
- **Some kind of [noun]** for a weak, mixed, or close nearest-neighbor match.

The indefinite article is generated from the actual rendered noun phrase; it is never omitted from the Unmistakably form.

Current fit gates remain theory-derived rather than population-calibrated: Unmistakably requires identity score >= .82 and margin >= .07; Some kind of applies below .72 or with margin < .04.

## Locked Source champion units

### Positivist
1. **H. L. A. Hart** — baseline social-rule/open-texture positivism.
2. **Jules Coleman / Wil Waluchow / Matthew Kramer** — Inclusive Positivism.
3. **Joseph Raz / Scott Shapiro / Andrei Marmor** — Exclusive Positivism.
4. **Tom Campbell / Jeremy Waldron / Frederick Schauer** — Normative/Ethical Positivism.

Kelsen, Austin, Gardner, Green, Himma, and related figures remain important lineage/further reading unless later work reveals a distinct judge-type gap.

### Interpretivist
5. **Ronald Dworkin / Nicos Stavropoulos** — central legal Interpretivism: institutional practice bears on law through the principles that best fit and justify it.

T.R.S. Allan remains important further reading pending the later Common-Law Constitutionalism / interpretive-method review.

### Natural Lawyer
6. **John Finnis / Mark Murphy / Robert P. George** — central modern Natural Law: objective moral order plus a substantial settlement, coordination, and institutional-authority role for positive law.

Aquinas, Blackstone, Jonathan Crowe, and related thinkers remain lineage/further reading.

### Fullerian
7. **Lon Fuller** — internal morality/principles of legality.

### Residual Non-Positivist branches
8. **Mark Greenberg** — Moral Impact Theory.
9. **Scott Hershovitz** — moral-practice / one-system nonpositivism.
10. **Gustav Radbruch / Philip Soper** — extreme-injustice threshold.

Robert Alexy remains primarily a Proportionalist champion rather than a duplicate Extreme-Injustice Source champion.

## Adaptive diagnostics

The global adaptive cap remains seven questions.

- **D-POS1** — Inclusive versus Exclusive Positivism.
- **D-POS2** — Normative/Ethical Positivism.
- **D-NP1** — Natural Law: objective morality plus positive-law settlement.
- **D-NP2** — Interpretivism: principles, fit, justification, and discovery rather than judicial creation.
- **D-NP4** — Moral Impact Theory / Greenberg.
- **D-NP5** — Moral-Practice nonpositivism / Hershovitz.
- **D-NP6** — Extreme-Injustice threshold / Radbruch-Soper.
- **D-NP3** — Fullerian internal legality.

The router stops probing competing Source theories once a broad noun or narrow branch is affirmatively established. Source probes also do not consume the diagnostic budget when a respondent is already squarely inside the Originalist refinement zone; Originalist source-sensitive champions use the 93-item core Source evidence.

## Reachability contract

The audit suite requires every locked Source champion unit to be separately reachable from literal 93-item answer vectors through the live adaptive router within the seven-question cap.

It also requires:

- Positivist to remain the public noun for Inclusive, Exclusive, and Normative branches.
- Interpretivist to be public while Dworkinian remains internal.
- Greenberg and Hershovitz to resolve as descriptive Non-Positivist modifiers rather than eponymous nouns.
- Radbruch/Soper to resolve as Extreme-Injustice Non-Positivist even when the respondent is otherwise comparatively source-positive.
- Fullerian to remain its own public noun.
- Generic fit output to include the correct indefinite article, e.g. **Unmistakably a Positivist** and **Unmistakably an Interpretivist**.
