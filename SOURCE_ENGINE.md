# Source-of-law classification engine

This file records the locked algorithmic architecture for the Source / nature-of-law result zone. Public explanatory copy can change later without changing this taxonomy.

## Public nouns

The classifier may publicly return:

- **Positivist**
- **Natural Lawyer**
- **Interpretivist**
- **Fullerian**
- **Non-Positivist** only when a distinctive nonpositivist subtype is affirmatively established below the noun layer. A merely nonpositive Source score is not enough.

The following remain internal/legacy compatibility records rather than public nouns:

- Inclusive Positivist
- Exclusive Positivist
- Dworkinian
- Moral Impact Theory
- Radbruchian / Extreme-Injustice theory

## Modifier layer

### Positivist

Substantive modifiers:

- **Inclusive Positivist** — a source-grounded legal system may incorporate genuine moral criteria into its rules of legal validity.
- **Exclusive Positivist** — legal validity must remain identifiable through social/source facts rather than moral merits themselves.
- **Normative Positivist** — source-based legality is defended as an institutional and democratic virtue that limits merits-based judicial lawmaking.

Normative Positivism is orthogonal to the Inclusive/Exclusive conceptual dispute. The public modifier engine shows the strongest supported substantive branch rather than forcing every Positivist into a complete partition.

### Non-Positivist subtype umbrella

Substantive descriptive modifiers:

- **Moral-Impact Non-Positivist** → Mark Greenberg.
- **Moral-Practice Non-Positivist** → Scott Hershovitz.
- **Extreme-Injustice Non-Positivist** → Gustav Radbruch / Philip Soper.

These modifiers deliberately describe the jurisprudential theory rather than repeating the champion's surname.

### Natural Lawyer / Interpretivist / Fullerian

No substantive modifier is currently locked. These nouns use the generic fit modifier unless later research reveals a genuine intermediate camp that changes the jurist type.

## Generic fit modifier

When no substantive modifier applies:

- **Unmistakably a [noun]** or **Unmistakably an [vowel-initial noun]** for an unusually clean, well-separated match.
- **[noun]** for the deliberately narrow middle band.
- **Some kind of [noun]** for a weak, mixed, or close nearest-neighbor match.

The indefinite article is generated from the actual rendered noun phrase; it is never omitted from the Unmistakably form.

Current fit gates remain theory-derived rather than population-calibrated: Unmistakably requires identity score >= .82 and margin >= .07; Some kind of applies below .72 or with margin < .04.

## Adaptive diagnostics

The global adaptive cap remains twelve questions (88 core + at most 12 adaptive, including quality checks). The response check is first; Realism is a candidate-sensitive probe rather than a universal opening chain.

- **D-POS1** — Inclusive versus Exclusive Positivism.
- **D-AUS1** — Austinian command/sovereign theory.
- **D-KEL1** — Kelsenian normative hierarchy/basic norm.
- **D-SHA1** — Shapiro Planning Theory.
- **D-POS2** — Normative/Ethical Positivism.
- **D-NP1** — Natural Law: objective morality plus positive-law settlement.
- **D-NP2** — Interpretivism: principles, fit, justification, and discovery rather than judicial creation.
- **D-NP4** — Moral Impact Theory / Greenberg.
- **D-NP5** — Moral-Practice nonpositivism / Hershovitz.
- **D-NL2** — Strong Natural Law / Michael Moore.
- **D-NP6** — Extreme-Injustice threshold / Radbruch-Soper.
- **D-NP3** — Fullerian internal legality.

The router stops speculative searches for competing Source theories once a real noun or narrow branch is affirmatively established. In particular, affirmed Fullerian internal legality ends the fallback search for Natural Law and Interpretivism when the core supplies no clue to either. Independently supported Source theories retain their targeted probes, including when Fullerian evidence is also present. If the respondent is nonpositive on the broad Source axis but rejects the diagnosable nonpositivist theories, Source alone does not manufacture a Non-Positivist noun; another established jurisprudential commitment wins, or the explicit Mixed fallback is used.

Source refinement is eligible when the Source family is live in the identity race or the Source score is extreme (at most 18 or at least 82). It also explores an unresolved Mixed result when the Source score lies in the existing nonpositive diagnostic zone (at most 38). This avoids a circular gate: Natural Law and distinctive nonpositivist theories need affirmative diagnostic evidence before they can enter the public identity race. Neutral Source scores do not unlock that exploration, and an already-established competing family keeps the ordinary candidate-aware gate. A secondary historical Time score never suppresses a live Source family; Originalist source-sensitive champions also retain their targeted Source-evidence refinement.

## Reachability contract

The audit suite requires every locked Source champion unit to be separately reachable from literal 88-item answer vectors through the live adaptive router within the twelve-question cap.

It also requires:

- Positivist to remain the public noun for Inclusive, Exclusive, and Normative branches.
- Interpretivist to be public while Dworkinian remains internal.
- Greenberg and Hershovitz to resolve as descriptive Non-Positivist modifiers rather than eponymous nouns.
- Radbruch/Soper to resolve as Extreme-Injustice Non-Positivist even when the respondent is otherwise comparatively source-positive.
- Fullerian to remain its own public noun.
- Generic fit output to include the correct indefinite article, e.g. **Unmistakably a Positivist** and **Unmistakably an Interpretivist**.



## Defining commitment, subtype, and whole-profile matching (revision)

The eight displayed axes are core-only. Diagnostic responses establish doctrines,
subtypes, comparative priority, and champion distinctions; they do not change axis
percentages. Axis extremity is provisional support, **not measured importance**.

Eligibility remains doctrine-specific. Among eligible nouns, the common comparison
is `.56 + .34 * coreSupport + .22 * netComparativePriority`, bounded to [0,1].
`coreSupport` uses the strongest eligible defining-axis support within that family;
individual nouns retain their existing defining axis or documented combination.
Within-family doctrinal specificity then resolves the form of that commitment;
these combinations remain theory-derived, not population-calibrated. Specificity
breaks within-support ties but no longer supplies a cross-family intercept bonus.
In particular, either sign of D-POS1 cannot promote Positivism's primary score.
CLC rights, living subtypes, and originalist subtypes use the same separation.
Redundant ancestor noun candidates do not count as independent alternatives.

D-PRI-{a}-{b} compares pairs in the ordered families source, time, method, posture,
structure, form, realism, and common_good. These 28 bank entries are alternatives, not 28 required
questions. A close live cross-family comparison can receive its appropriate probe.
The wording explicitly allows compatible commitments and asks which more
characteristically defines the respondent. Neither/skip supplies no priority
advantage; skipped evidence stays absent. Unresolved close comparisons are exposed
in `identityPriority.unresolved`, never presented as measured certainty.

The scheduler re-evaluates decisions each turn. Quality comes first. Material noun
priority and plausible champion prerequisites compete with champion distinctions;
champion changes receive the largest decision-sensitivity weight. It evaluates all
seven answers plus skip. These are heuristic sensitivities, not probabilities or
expected information gain. Triggered follow-ups have a small bonus and remain
subject to family repetition cost. Missing thinker requirements can open affordable
one/two-requirement discovery paths without bypassing doctrinal Source exclusions.
The router does not reserve a fixed noun/champion quota or guarantee five Realism
questions. Existing source theory exclusion rules remain operative.

Champion matching retains one output. Independently supported secondary noun
families may supply a thinker through their existing identity/bridge mappings;
a primary noun is not an exhaustive worldview. No thinker axis or biographical
position was invented. Required traits and cross-cutting requirements still apply.

`champions[0].evidence` records:
- `coverage`: proportion of substantial respondent axis support represented by the
  thinker's existing axis model (substantial means strength >= .35).
- `modeledAxes`, `supportedAxes`, `unknownAxes`: represented domains and important
  missing domains; supportedAxes means coverage, not guaranteed agreement.
- `knownDisagreements`: represented important axes at least 40 points apart.
- `traitDisagreements`, `supportedTraits`: affirmed anti/signature evidence.
- `scope`: `modeled-profile` or `specific-affinity` for zero-axis records.

Missing coverage is uncertainty, never a fabricated disagreement or neutral thinker
position. Match ranking discounts missing coverage by up to .22. Zero-axis matches
use a bounded affinity score and cannot exceed .69, including remedial overrides.
They remain selectable when evidence supports their distinctive theory; the bound
prevents isolated specialty agreement from receiving an apparent strong broad-fit
score. High confidence additionally requires coverage >= .70, no known axis or
anti-trait disagreement, score >= .76, and margin >= .055. These are transparent
heuristic guardrails, not validated probabilities. Catalog gaps require historical
research, not extra respondent questions.

Focused regression: `node scripts/audit-primary-commitment.mjs`. Existing literal
fixtures supply null for new priority questions because their old answer plans did
not specify comparative importance. Historical frozen observations are not rewritten.
Modifier/phrase inventory is unchanged by this classifier revision.

### Bounded discovery and execution cost

A missing doctrine cannot be required to win a shortlist before its identifying
question is asked. Moderate Method scores (30–70) can make an unconfirmed method
family worth a comparative-priority probe. Discretion evidence or a non-extreme
whole profile can do the same for descriptive Realism. Affirmed relative centrality
then opens the relevant existing pluralism/Legal Process or Realism compatibility
questions at material-uncertainty priority. Importance alone never supplies those
doctrines. Skipped/rejected doctrine controls in the focused audit enforce that
boundary; the algorithm has no persona names or fixed discovery quota.

Per-call caching reuses equivalent hypothetical trait states and immutable catalog
lineages; all seven answers and skip remain represented. The focused audit prints
actual maximum next-question computation time for six discovery routes, without
asserting a hardware-independent latency promise.

### Explicit-primary safeguards

A strongly affirmed direct family preference (magnitude at least 2/3) orders
already-eligible families before the common support score, provided the preference
graph is acyclic. Winning against a third family cannot cancel a direct defeat.
Weak preferences retain proportional score influence; neutral and skipped items do
not create an ordering edge. Cyclic comparisons fall back deterministically and
set `identityPriority.conflictingComparisons` and `unresolved`, without branding
the whole response incoherent or inventing a consistent ranking.

Where comparative evidence establishes primary importance, a champion cannot win
solely through an incompatible secondary family. Existing cross-cutting bridges
also need actual modeled primary-domain or primary-doctrine signature coverage.
A low-theory catch-all remains possible with affirmative case-by-case/anti-grand-
theory evidence, not merely because some axis scores are moderate. This preserves
supported whole-profile matching without allowing a Source-only prototype to
represent explicitly primary Originalism. Special remedial overrides obey the
same explicit-primary coverage guard.

The same priority-summary helper is passed to final and hypothetical title
assembly so unresolved/cyclic priority cannot acquire stronger fit wording during
routing than at final output. Late latent-family discovery requires at least three
remaining slots; it must not displace an already-available champion discriminator
with an importance question whose confirmation chain cannot fit.


### Common-good priority and provisional champion compatibility

Common-Good Constitutionalism retains its historical catalog family `time` for
title inventory compatibility, but its routing priority domain is `common_good`.
The seven additional pair comparisons ask about directing public power toward
the community's common good, never infer this from wanting constitutional meaning
to change over time, and become live only after the substantive doctrine exists.
All original priority prompts are unchanged. Old unplanned comparisons are skipped.

When defining priority remains unresolved, a secondary compatible champion with
at least 0.15 more salient-axis coverage than the best direct candidate and no
known axis or trait disagreement loses only the small 0.025 canonicality
distinction, rather than 0.09. Otherwise the normal distinction remains. The full 0.09
distinction remains for a clearly separated inferred primary; the explicit-primary
representation guard remains mandatory. This lets modeled whole-profile agreement
outweigh a provisional noun while preserving known disagreements and prerequisites.

The final literal Vermeule fixture retains every original answer and its exact
champion assertion. Its provisional noun changes from Common-Good Constitutionalist
to Purposivist, with CGC retained as an eligible alternative. Vermeule covers all
four salient axes; Breyer leaves Source unmodeled. A separate prospective literal
variant explicitly prioritizes the common good and reaches CGC/Vermeule within
100 questions. This is an additional noun migration, not an invented Time answer.

A hard primary-representation champion guard requires material evidence on both
sides: comparative-priority magnitude at least 0.5 and substantive candidate basis
at least 0.35. Weak self-reported priority or a slight substantive tilt retains
independently evidenced adjacent matches. Existing cross-cutting mappings with
modeled defining-domain or signature support remain allowed even under the guard;
sharing the noun label is not required.
