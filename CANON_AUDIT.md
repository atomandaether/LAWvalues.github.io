# LAWvalues Canon Audit

Purpose: decide which jurisprudential names belong in the **middle identity slot**, which figures should be **champions only**, and which belong in the **background canon** but should not drive classification.

The middle slot must use the narrowest real label that the quiz can actually justify. Historical importance alone is not enough. A thinker or school earns a direct result identity only when the position is (1) canonical or at least clearly established, (2) distinctive, (3) diagnosable from LAWvalues, and (4) better than a broader real label.

## Canon filter

For every candidate, ask:

1. **Canonicality** — Is this an established school, theory, or major jurisprudential position?
2. **Distinctiveness** — Does it describe a response pattern that is meaningfully different from neighboring labels?
3. **Diagnosability** — Can existing questions, plus at most a few adaptive diagnostics, distinguish it?
4. **Naming fit** — Is there a real public-facing label that can occupy the middle slot?
5. **Coverage value** — Does it fill a real hole in the result space?
6. **No-overclaim rule** — If we cannot distinguish the specific subtype, fall back to the broader real label.

# Tier 1 — Direct middle-slot identities

These are real labels worth identifying directly when the evidence is strong enough.

## Nature / source of law

### Positivist
**Status:** Core direct label.

Modern legal positivism is one of the central positions in jurisprudence. Hart and Raz are among its canonical modern architects, with inclusive and exclusive variants worth distinguishing adaptively if needed.

**Adaptive children:**
- Inclusive Positivist / Hartian
- Exclusive Positivist / Razian

**Do not infer from:** textualism, originalism, deference, or rules alone.

### Dworkinian
**Status:** Direct label when the full pattern is present.

Prefer **Dworkinian** over generic “Interpretivist” when the respondent specifically endorses principles as part of existing law, fit-and-justification reasoning, and the idea that a hard-case answer can be discovered rather than newly made.

“Interpretivist” remains a fallback if the person is clearly in that family but not specifically Dworkinian.

### Natural Lawyer
**Status:** Core direct label.

Use for a genuine natural-law pattern, especially where objective practical/moral reason matters to law but positive law retains a major settlement function.

**Adaptive child / champion refinement:**
- Finnisian, only if we have unusually precise evidence.
- Natural-Law Originalist when the Time pattern independently supports originalism.

### Radbruchian
**Status:** Conditional direct label.

Worth using only where the respondent has the distinctive threshold view: ordinary injustice does not necessarily erase legality, but sufficiently extreme injustice can.

This should not be inferred merely from generic Non-positivism.

### Fullerian
**Status:** Conditional / later.

Fuller’s “internal morality of law” is canonical, but the current bank does not directly test publicity, prospectivity, congruence, intelligibility, etc. Do not classify anyone as Fullerian without a targeted diagnostic.

### Alexyan
**Status:** Conditional direct label, probably rare.

Potentially useful where strong non-positivism combines with claim-to-correctness reasoning and proportionality. Do not use merely because someone likes balancing.

## Statutory interpretation / legal method

### Textualist
**Status:** Core direct label.

Established, broad, and directly tested. Contextual textualism should be treated as a subtype, not as Open Construction.

### Purposivist
**Status:** Core direct label.

Established and directly tested.

### Legal-Process Theorist
**Status:** Direct label if the Hart-and-Sacks combination is present.

Not just “uses purpose.” It should require purposive reasoning plus institutional competence / allocation / settlement themes. The label is real even if it is multiword.

### Pragmatist
**Status:** Direct label when consequences and workability dominate across domains.

Use cautiously. A respondent who merely balances one doctrine is not automatically a legal pragmatist.

---

## Constitutional time

### Originalist
**Status:** Core direct label and fallback parent.

Do not guess a subtype when diagnostics cannot distinguish it.

**Adaptive children worth identifying:**
- Public-Meaning Originalist
- Original-Intent Originalist
- Original-Methods Originalist
- Framework Originalist

The first three are recognized competing theories inside originalism; Framework Originalism is a real Balkin label.

**Important:** Principle-oriented versus expected-applications-heavy originalism may be better expressed as a front modifier unless there is a recognized narrower identity we can defend.

### Living Constitutionalist
**Status:** Core direct label.

Use when the respondent affirmatively permits constitutional content itself to evolve, not merely new applications of fixed meaning.

### Common-Law Constitutionalist
**Status:** Core direct label.

This is a real and distinctive theory, especially associated with David Strauss. It should require the combination of constitutional development through precedent/practice and strong settlement/stability themes.

---

## Judicial review / democratic authority

### Thayerian
**Status:** Conditional direct label.

Real and distinctive: very strong judicial deference / clear-error rule. It is a theory of adjudicative deference, not a general theory of interpretation.

Unlock only for very high Majoritarian / clear-error profiles.

### Waldronian
**Status:** Conditional direct label.

Use only where the respondent’s majoritarianism is specifically grounded in democratic legitimacy under reasonable rights disagreement and functioning institutions. Do not use for generic judicial restraint.

### Popular Constitutionalist
**Status:** Conditional direct label.

This is a real theory associated especially with Larry Kramer and Mark Tushnet. It should require more than “very Majoritarian”: the respondent must reject judicial supremacy or give the people/political process an independent role in constitutional meaning.

### Departmentalist
**Status:** Conditional direct label.

Use where the respondent rejects judicial supremacy because the political branches retain independent interpretive authority, without necessarily endorsing broad popular constitutional development.

### Elyan
**Status:** Conditional direct label.

Possible only if diagnostics show the representation-reinforcement pattern: ordinary democratic deference plus heightened intervention when the political process malfunctions or excludes groups.

### Bickelian
**Status:** Conditional direct label.

Possible where passive virtues / avoidance are unusually central. Do not use merely for Narrow Remedies or general restraint.

### Minimalist
**Status:** Direct label when narrow decisions / avoidance are the dominant identity.

“Judicial Minimalist” is a real family label. It may be better than Bickelian when the exact Bickelian theory is not established.

---

## Institutional structure / decisional form

### Formalist
**Status:** Core direct label.

Use when separation-of-powers formalism or broad legal formalism is genuinely the respondent’s dominant identity. Because “formalism” has multiple legal uses, body copy should explain whether the fit is structural, rule-based, or both.

### Functionalist
**Status:** Core direct label.

Real separation-of-powers category. Use when institutional workability / overlap is especially distinctive.

### Proportionalist
**Status:** Conditional direct label.

Potentially useful for a respondent whose Form answers strongly support proportionality-style balancing. If the evidence is merely generic standards/balancing, use a modifier instead.

### Legal Realist
**Status:** Special diagnostic override identity.

Realism is the only non-axis identity currently worth building as a separate probe/override because the eight axes do not measure the descriptive claim that legal doctrine underdetermines hard cases and that judicial behavior is substantially shaped by extra-doctrinal factors.

**Champion preference:** Llewellyn / Frank are cleaner American Realist exemplars than treating Holmes as the whole school; Holmes is an important precursor.

# Tier 2 — Champions / subtype exemplars, not default middle labels

These figures are highly useful, but the result should normally use a real broader identity and mention the figure as a champion or relative.

## Hart
Champion of modern legal positivism and especially social-practice / rule-of-recognition thinking.

Use **Positivist** or, after diagnostics, **Inclusive Positivist / Hartian**.

## Raz
Champion of exclusive/source-based positivism.

Use **Exclusive Positivist** or **Razian** only after a genuine inclusive/exclusive diagnostic.

## Finnis
Canonical modern natural-law champion.

Use **Natural Lawyer** by default. “Finnisian” is reserved for a precise match.

## Pojanowski
Excellent champion for the unusual combination Natural Law + Originalism + respect for posited settlement.

Not foundational enough to make “Pojanowskian” a normal result label.

## Solum
Major champion for Public-Meaning Originalism, fixation/constraint, and interpretation/construction distinctions.

Use the theory name rather than “Solumian.”

## McGinnis & Rappaport
Primary champions for Original-Methods Originalism.

Use **Original-Methods Originalist**.

## Balkin
Primary champion for Framework Originalism.

Use **Framework Originalist**.

## David Strauss
Primary champion for Common-Law Constitutionalism.

Use the theory name.

## Scalia
Champion of textualism, public-meaning originalism, separation formalism, and rule-oriented judging.

Do not use “Scalian” as a broad identity because his bundle spans several independently measured dimensions.

## John Manning
Champion / major theorist of modern textualism.

Use **Textualist**.

## Hart & Sacks
Champions of Legal Process.

Use **Legal-Process Theorist** when the full institutional/purposive package fits.

## Thomas / Paulsen
Champion pair for correctness-first / precedent-skeptical originalism.

Do **not** make “Thomas-Paulsen” a middle identity. Use:
- **Originalist** / relevant originalist subtype
- plus a correctness-first / precedent-breaking modifier or phrase.

Paulsen’s theory is especially clear that inconsistent precedent corrupts the underlying interpretive theory.

## Barrett
Champion for precedent-sensitive originalism / stare decisis as a referee among competing constitutional visions.

Not a separate school.

## Kozel
Champion for theories of precedent, impersonality, continuity, and settlement.

Not a separate middle identity unless later scholarship gives us a recognized label worth using.

## Schauer
Champion for rules / formalism / constraint.

Usually better as a modifier around another real middle identity than as “Schauerian,” unless the response is an unusually exact rule-theory match.

## Peter Strauss
Champion of functional separation-of-powers analysis.

Use **Functionalist**.

## Gary Lawson
Champion of strong separation-of-powers formalism and formalist originalism.

Use **Formalist** or the relevant Originalist subtype with a structural-formalist modifier.

## Fiss / Chayes
Champions of the structural-reform / public-law model.

**Naming rule:** “Structural Reform” is **not** a middle-slot identity because it does not fit the naming grammar and is a model of adjudication/remedy rather than a natural person-label.

Use:
- normally a modifier/phrase such as systemic / institution-reforming;
- **Fissian** only if the respondent’s remedial profile very specifically matches Fiss’s structural-reform conception;
- **Chayesian** only if the broader public-law litigation / managerial-judge model is specifically identifiable.

Do not force a remedies-heavy respondent into an invented “Structuralist” or “Remedialist” label.

## Samuel Bray
Champion for plaintiff-specific / historically traditional equity and opposition to universal injunctions.

Usually modifier/phrase material, not a standalone middle identity.

## Alexander Bickel
Champion for passive virtues / counter-majoritarian difficulty.

Use **Bickelian** only after a distinctive avoidance/passive-virtues pattern.

## John Hart Ely
Champion for representation reinforcement.

Use **Elyan** only if the process-malfunction conditional is actually diagnosed.

## Larry Kramer / Mark Tushnet
Champions for Popular Constitutionalism.

Use the theory name, not the person names, unless we later identify a genuinely different Kramer/Tushnet subtype.

# Tier 3 — Background canon / historical relatives

These figures matter intellectually, but historical stature does not by itself justify a result label.

## Blackstone
**Status:** Background canon; possible champion for some historical combinations.

Blackstone is unquestionably central to Anglo-American legal history and articulated natural-law ideas within the common-law tradition. But “Blackstonian” does not presently map to one clean LAWvalues identity. His thought bundles natural law, common-law tradition, institutional settlement, and eighteenth-century constitutional assumptions that cut across our modern axes.

**Conclusion:** include Blackstone in the intellectual-relative/champion database. Do not create a Blackstonian result unless we later add diagnostics for a genuinely distinctive Blackstone-like position.

This is the model for handling famous thinkers who are historically canonical but not diagnostically useful.

## Aquinas
Foundational historical natural-law source; background ancestor rather than ordinary modern classification label.

## Austin / Bentham
Foundational modern positivist history. Useful lineage, but current respondents are better classified through modern Positivist / Hartian / Razian distinctions.

## Kelsen
Major positivist architect. Keep in canon/champion database. Consider direct classification only if LAWvalues ever adds enough diagnostics to identify a specifically Kelsenian normative-system view.

## Holmes
Canonical precursor to American Legal Realism and judicial restraint traditions. Important champion/relative, but Llewellyn and Frank are cleaner if the specific target is American Realism.

## Hand / Frankfurter / Brandeis
Important exemplars of judicial restraint/deference traditions. Usually champions, not distinct result identities.

## Warren Court
Institutional champion for rights-protective judicial review and some broad-remedy traditions; not a middle identity.

## Israeli Supreme Court
Potential institutional champion for a rights/proportionality/judicial-review combination, but only after specifying era/doctrine. Never use it as a vague synonym for “activist court.”

## German proportionality tradition / Federal Constitutional Court
Potential institutional champion for proportionality-oriented results.

## Canadian dialogic / weak-form review
Potential institutional champion for a dialogic middle position on judicial review if adaptive diagnostics identify it.

# Candidates considered but currently outside scope

These are real schools but should not receive diagnostics merely because they exist.

## Critical Legal Studies
Important, but requires propositions about hierarchy, ideology, contradiction, and politics that go beyond the eight-axis jurisprudential project. Do not add unless LAWvalues broadens scope.

## Law & Economics
Important, but welfare/efficiency analysis is a substantive normative method that does not explain enough of the current 93-item architecture to justify a separate classification tree.

## Feminist jurisprudence / Critical Race Theory
Important legal-intellectual traditions, but adding them would broaden the quiz toward substantive critical theory rather than the current institutional/interpretive design.

## Republican / anti-domination constitutionalism
Potentially meaningful but currently closer to substantive political theory than a gap in the LAWvalues classification system.

# Diagnostic priorities after the audit

## High priority

1. **Originalist subtype router**
   - Public Meaning vs Original Intent
   - Original Methods if still ambiguous
   - Framework/construction if needed

2. **Non-positivist router**
   - Dworkinian vs Natural Lawyer
   - Radbruchian threshold
   - Fullerian only if we choose to add procedural-legality diagnostics

3. **Positivist router**
   - Inclusive/Hartian vs Exclusive/Razian

4. **Extreme-Majoritarian router**
   - Thayerian
   - Waldronian
   - Popular Constitutionalist
   - Departmentalist
   - Elyan only where process-malfunction pattern appears

5. **Legal Realism probe**
   - one universal soft probe
   - additional questions only if the respondent latches on

## Medium priority

6. **Legal Process vs generic Purposivism/Pragmatism**
7. **Bickelian passive virtues vs generic Minimalism**
8. **Alexyan / Proportionalist precision if Form results prove too generic**

## No extra diagnostics currently needed

- Common-Law Constitutionalism
- separation Formalist / Functionalist
- strong Rules orientation
- systemic vs narrow Remedies
- correctness-first precedent posture
- generic Textualism / Purposivism

The current bank already has enough direct evidence for those.

# Naming correction: systemic-remedy identities

Do not use **Structural Reform** as the middle slot.

Preferred hierarchy:

1. If systemic remedies are merely one major feature:
   - express them in the **front modifier** or **trailing phrase**.

2. If the respondent very specifically matches the Fiss structural-reform theory:
   - allow **Fissian** as the middle identity.

3. If the respondent specifically matches Chayes’s public-law / managerial-judge model:
   - allow **Chayesian** as the middle identity.

4. Never invent:
   - Structuralist
   - Structural-Reformist
   - Remedialist

unless later research shows a real established jurisprudential usage matching what we mean.

# Core principle from the audit

A famous thinker can be:

- a **direct identity**,
- a **champion of a real identity**,
- a **champion of a subtype/combination**,
- or merely **background canon**.

The question is never “is this person important?”

The question is:

> **Does this name or school describe a distinctive response pattern that LAWvalues can honestly identify better than the alternatives?**
