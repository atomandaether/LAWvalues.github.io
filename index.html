What is LAWvalues?

This test is a legal jurisprudence quiz that assigns percentages for sixteen legal-philosophy values across eight axes. You will be presented with statements about legal interpretation and judicial philosophy and answer from Strongly Agree to Strongly Disagree. Each question affects one axis only. There are 48 questions.

What are the sixteen values?

There are eight independent axes of legal jurisprudence, each with two opposing values:

POSITIVIST

Law's authority comes from validly enacted sources like constitutions, statutes, and rules. Judges apply those sources as law even when the result is morally objectionable.

SOURCE

NON-POSITIVIST

Legal authority is bounded or partly constituted by higher norms, moral principles, and fundamental rights that positive sources cannot exhaust.

TEXTUALIST

Meaning turns on the communicative content of enacted text. Syntax, structure, and linguistic canons guide reading; unenacted purpose has limited force.

CONSTRAINT

OPEN CONSTRUCTION

Text is read broadly to effectuate its aims and close loopholes. Purpose, consequences, and context legitimately shape legal effect.

HISTORICAL

Meaning is fixed at adoption and informed by original public usage, early practice, and inherited legal tradition.

TIME

LIVING

Constitutional content may develop through precedent, changed facts, accumulated experience, and contemporary understandings.

STABILITY

Precedent carries heavy weight to preserve reliance, continuity, predictability, and institutional legitimacy.

PRECEDENT

OVERRULING

Seriously wrong, incoherent, or unworkable precedents should be discarded despite disruption and reliance costs.

NARROW

Remedies should be party-specific, severable, incremental, and no broader than necessary to resolve the dispute.

REMEDIES

SYSTEMIC

Broad injunctions, structural decrees, and continuing oversight are acceptable when needed to end pervasive violations.

MAJORITARIAN

Courts generally defer to elected branches and democratic outputs, intervening for reasonably clear legal violations.

POSTURE

COUNTER-MAJORITARIAN

Courts independently and searchingly protect rights and constitutional structure, including against sustained majority will.

FORMALIST

Separation of powers is enforced through bright lines and prescribed forms. Each branch must act through constitutionally assigned procedures.

SEPARATION

FUNCTIONALIST

Pragmatic overlap is acceptable when accountability, checks, and each branch's core capacity remain intact.

RULES

Bright lines and categories promote notice, consistency, administrability, and constraint even at the cost of imperfect fit.

FORM

BALANCING

Standards and proportionality permit closer fit to facts and values even at the cost of discretion and uncertainty.
