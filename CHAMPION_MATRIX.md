This document answers a different question from the axis definitions:

> **Once LAWvalues knows the respondent's best real jurisprudential identity, which actual thinker, judge, court, or tradition is the best exemplar of this particular version of it?**

There should **not** be one permanent champion per identity. A champion is a second-stage match inside the identity family.

## Core rule

1. Pick the **narrowest real middle identity** the evidence supports.
2. Only then compare champions that are compatible with that identity or with the respondent's strongest remaining traits.
3. Prefer the champion whose **whole measured signature** matches the respondent, not the most famous name.
4. Require **marginal explanatory value**: a champion belongs in the selectable pool only if they cover a region of LAWvalues space that the real identity and existing champions do not already cover just as well.
5. Prune redundancy. If Thayer already names the deference theory, Frankfurter/Hand/Brandeis do not each need equal champion status unless another measured trait makes one of them uniquely informative.
6. Do not force a champion when the fit is weak or tied.
7. A historical ancestor can appear as lineage without being the closest champion.

This is a **set-cover problem, not a fame contest**. The pool should be as small as possible while still covering genuinely different measured combinations.

This is why:
- Solum is not "the Originalist."
- Scalia is not "the Textualist" for every textualist.
- Holmes is not "the Legal Realist" for every realist.
- Finnis is not automatically every nonpositivist.
- Fiss can be a remedial champion even when the respondent's middle identity is something else.

## Resolution levels

### Level 0 — lineage only
The figure is historically important but the quiz does not test the distinctive theory closely enough.

Examples: Blackstone, Aquinas, Austin/Bentham, Kelsen for Pure Theory, Holmes as a Realist precursor.

### Level 1 — family champion
The respondent clearly belongs to a broad identity and the champion is a canonical exemplar.

Examples:
- Positivist -> Hart
- Natural Lawyer -> Finnis
- Pragmatist -> Posner
- Common-Law Constitutionalist -> David Strauss by default; Harlan II or Cooke only with their distinctive evidence

### Level 2 — subtype champion
Extra axis information makes a particular champion clearly better.

Examples:
- Textualist + public meaning + Rules + structural formalism -> Scalia
- Textualist without the broader Scalia bundle -> Manning
- Originalist + correctness-first precedent -> Thomas or Paulsen
- Originalist + stronger settlement/stare decisis -> Barrett
- Natural Law + Originalism + positive-law settlement -> Pojanowski/Walsh

### Level 3 — diagnostic champion
Adaptive diagnostics identify a theory precisely enough that the champion can be very specific.

Examples:
- Original-Methods Originalist -> McGinnis/Rappaport
- Framework Originalist -> Balkin
- Public-Meaning + principle/construction emphasis -> Solum
- Confirmed Legal Realist + general law-in-action orientation -> Llewellyn
- Confirmed Legal Realist + psychological/fact skepticism -> Frank, if later diagnostics support that distinction
- Sociological Jurist + source-gap Free Scientific Research -> François Gény
- Common-Law Constitutionalist + living tradition / ordered liberty -> Harlan II
- Common-Law Constitutionalist + fundamental common-law limits on legislative supremacy -> Lord Cooke
- Living Constitutionalist + democratic constitutional development -> Post/Siegel
- Living Constitutionalist + transformative constitutional project -> Klare/Langa
- Inclusive Positivism + Common-Law Constitutionalism -> Waluchow
- Political Process Jurist + responsive, dysfunction-specific calibration -> Rosalind Dixon
- Judicial Minimalist + passive virtues / strategic avoidance -> Alexander Bickel

## Champion guardrails

Champion matching is noun-conditioned and whole-profile-sensitive. Incompatible global fallback is prohibited; a cross-cutting champion needs an explicit affirmative bridge; and a sparse champion profile is modestly penalized when it leaves a respondent's strongest high-salience commitments unexplained. If no affirmative public school is established, the internal **Mixed Jurisprudence** fallback uses O'Connor rather than pretending the respondent is an Interpretive Pluralist.

## Important figures that should not automatically become middle labels

### Blackstone
Blackstone is canonically important, but the current quiz does not isolate one "Blackstonian" package. He is best treated as lineage / possible champion for traditional natural-law + common-law + stability combinations.

### Kelsen
Kelsen is a central positivist architect, but the Pure Theory's distinctive normative hierarchy/basic-norm structure is not measured. Keep him in the canon, not as a default respondent label.

### Holmes
Holmes is a crucial precursor to Realism and a practicing Thayerian. He stays useful only because the **cross-combination** Realism + strong democratic deference is measurable. He should not occupy a separate slot merely as another Thayer follower.

### Hugo Black
Black earns consideration for a different reason from Frankfurter or Brandeis: he occupies a distinctive measured bundle that Thayer does not absorb. His constitutional style paired unusually literal/text-bound reasoning and categorical rights with aggressive enforcement of enumerated rights. LAWvalues can see much of that through Text/Rules/anti-discretion plus Counter-majoritarian rights protection. Total incorporation is historically central to Black but is not a LAWvalues matching trait unless a future diagnostic directly tests it.

### Brian Leiter
Leiter belongs in the champion pool because he adds **marginal conceptual coverage**, not because he is another famous Realist. His work reconstructs American Legal Realism as a descriptive theory of adjudication tied to naturalized jurisprudence and argues for a close relationship between Realism and hard/positivist theories of law. A respondent who is both a confirmed Legal Realist and strongly Positivist is therefore meaningfully closer to Leiter than to a generic Llewellyn champion.

### Frankfurter / Hand / Brandeis
Important judicial exemplars of restraint, but mostly **lineage rather than selectable champions** for present purposes. Thayer already names the clear-error/deference theory. Add one of these judges back only if a second measured dimension creates a distinctive profile that Thayer does not already explain.

### Bork
Bork matters historically to old originalism and democratic restraint, especially original-intent rhetoric. But he should not be added merely as another famous Originalist. If LAWvalues affirmatively identifies Original-Intent Originalism and needs a champion, Bork is a plausible historical/subtype candidate; otherwise he adds little beyond the theory label and the existing Originalist champion set.

### Easterbrook
Do not add by default. Once Law & Economics is deliberately outside scope, Easterbrook's remaining text/rules/constraint profile is largely redundant with Scalia/Manning for the dimensions LAWvalues measures.

### Scalia
Scalia is highly useful precisely because his signature crosses multiple LAWvalues axes: textualism, public-meaning originalism, rules, democratic anti-discretion, and separation formalism. That makes him a **great champion for a specific bundle and a bad universal champion for any one component**.

### Remedies and the one champion slot
Remedies do not create a public noun and do not receive a separate secondary champion.

- **Bhagwati/Iyer** remain selectable because PIL/social-action judging is a multi-axis jurisprudential package.
- **Fiss** remains selectable only through an extreme whole-profile structural-reform gate and can displace a noun-compatible champion only when that package is genuinely more explanatory overall.
- **Chayes, Bray, Sohoni/Frost, Monaghan, and Levinson** are lineage/result-explanation only and never compete for the user's single champion slot.

## Champion matching rule

A future classifier should assign each champion:
- eligible middle identities,
- positive signature traits,
- anti-traits,
- role: primary / subtype / historical / institutional.

After the middle identity is selected:

`champion_fit = matched_signature_information - contradicted_signature_information`

The actual implementation should weight **respondent trait importance**, not count tags equally.

Then:
- require at least two distinctive matched traits for a personal champion;
- do not select a champion whose anti-traits are among the respondent's strongest traits;
- always return exactly one champion unit for a valid result;
- close races reduce confidence but never produce a second champion;
- institutional or grouped champion units are allowed when they are more accurate than a single person.

## Why the catalog is deliberately not exhaustive

LAWvalues is not trying to classify every real legal movement. A school belongs only if it helps explain the current instrument.

Intentionally out of scope unless the project broadens:
- Critical Legal Studies
- Law & Economics as a separate identity
- feminist jurisprudence
- Critical Race Theory
- republican/anti-domination theory

They are real and important. They simply ask different questions from the present instrument.

## Important gap discovered by the audit: Intentionalism

The earlier catalog overemphasized Originalism while omitting a major statutory-interpretation theory: **Intentionalism**. Mainstream legal-interpretation surveys treat textualism, intentionalism, purposivism, and originalism as leading theories.

LAWvalues currently has evidence relevant to intent, but not enough to cleanly distinguish:
- "follow what the lawmakers actually intended"
from
- "identify an objective statutory purpose and carry it out."

The adaptive diagnostic bank therefore includes an Intentionalist/Purposivist tie-breaker.

## Realism's special status

Realism is the only presently approved identity that intentionally sits **outside** the eight axes.

Why:
- It is substantially descriptive: how much doctrine actually determines hard cases.
- A respondent can normatively favor Rules or Balancing, Originalism or Living Constitutionalism, Narrow or Systemic Remedies, and still be a Realist about actual judicial behavior.
- Therefore no extreme axis result reliably unlocks Realism.

The design is:
1. one soft Realism probe for everyone;
2. if the respondent agrees, two stronger probes;
3. only a strong pattern permits **Legal Realist** to override the normal middle identity;
4. the front modifier and trailing phrase still carry the respondent's normative commitments.

That allows results such as a rule-oriented Legal Realist or a rights-protective Legal Realist without pretending Realism settles those questions.


## Redundancy examples

The following illustrates the marginal-coverage rule:

- **Thayer** can absorb generic Frankfurter/Hand/Brandeis-style deference.
- **Scalia** stays because Textualism + public meaning + Rules + structural formalism is a distinctive cross-axis bundle.
- **Hugo Black** stays because text/rule absolutism + aggressive enumerated-rights enforcement occupies a very different region from Thayer and Scalia.
- **Llewellyn** stays as the broad mature Legal Realist.
- **Leiter** stays because Realism + Positivism/Naturalism is a distinctive modern theoretical combination.
- **Frank** is lineage unless LAWvalues adds fact-skepticism / psychology diagnostics.
- **Posner** stays because Pragmatism is a direct identity; his Law & Economics work is not what earns the slot.
- **Bork** is only useful if Original Intent is actually diagnosed.
- **Easterbrook** is presently redundant after excluding Law & Economics.

This is the answer to "why this person and not the next famous judge?" The burden is on every additional champion to explain a **new measured region**, not merely to deserve a place in a jurisprudence textbook.
