## Intellectual distinction

Practical reasoning combines legal considerations without a universally fixed hierarchy. Dynamic statutory interpretation additionally gives developments during a statute's life legitimate interpretive significance. Neither belief entails the other. The dynamic question distinguishes changing legal interpretation from applying fixed meaning to new facts. It assumes a genuine interpretive choice, not permission to ignore clear text. It does not require Calabresi's exceptional obsolescence or legislative-second-look thesis.

Primary sources:
- William N. Eskridge Jr., Dynamic Statutory Interpretation, 135 University of Pennsylvania Law Review 1479 (1987): https://openyls.law.yale.edu/entities/publication/551a6cf6-ed04-44a0-9af1-1d09ed4d6c5d
